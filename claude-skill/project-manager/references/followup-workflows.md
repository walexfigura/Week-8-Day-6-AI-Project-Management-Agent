# Follow-up Workflows

## Before drafting, answer all of these
1. Does the task actually need an update? (overdue, blocked, approaching, stale, projected late)
2. Is it blocked by an upstream task? Then ask the upstream owner instead.
3. Is there already a recent update (≤ 48 h) in the task, Slack or email that answers it?
4. Has someone already asked, with a reply still pending?
5. Is another person already handling it?
6. Do the frequency rules allow it?
7. Which channel fits?
8. What single question gets a date or decision?

## Frequency rules (defaults; adjust per team)
- At most one open question per person per task.
- Wait at least one working day for a reply before following up again.
- No more than 2 follow-ups to one person per day.
- After 2 unanswered attempts, escalate to the project lead instead of reminding again.
- Only send during working hours (9:00–17:30, Mon–Fri, no holidays). Hold anything approved outside them.
- Respect exemptions (vacation, offsite).

## One blocker, one conversation
James and Kevin both wait on Sarah, so ask Sarah. After she answers (and the user approves), send James and Kevin a short heads-up with the new estimate.

## Follow-up log (how to "remember" without a database)
At the end of a session, offer a log like the one below for the user to save and paste back next time:

| ID | Task | To | Channel | Status | Sent | Reply | Next allowed | Commitment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FU-1 | TASK-002 | Sarah | Slack | sent (approved) | Mar 10 09:30 | received 09:55 | - | Estimate Wed Mar 11 |

Status values: drafted, awaiting approval, approved, sent, replied, closed, escalated.
